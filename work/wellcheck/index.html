Summary

At Mekorot, Israel's national water company, I was the sole designer of WellCheck, the field app behind the daily work that keeps water in the tap for everyone in Israel, and of a few more products, including other apps and data platforms. In each one I owned the UX process, from meeting users to handing designs to developers.

Mekorot is a long-established organization, and much of its daily work still runs the way it always has: on paper, in spreadsheets and over email. My work was part of its move to digital tools, a shift that asks people to change habits they have relied on for years, in work that can't stop while they do.

This case follows one flow: receiving chlorine deliveries for drinking-water treatment, a safety check that used to live on paper and WhatsApp. The inspector now completes a guided check at the truck, a deviation can't slip through quietly, and the report reaches the company's systems even without signal.

Context

The field app behind the tap

Mekorot runs the facilities that pump, treat and deliver water across Israel. WellCheck is the app its field teams carry through that work, almost a parent app for the whole operation.

Block inspectors, maintenance teams and employees approved for specific tasks all work in it. They read meters, log water levels, handle work orders and fault reports, and receive deliveries. Each inspector sees only the facilities assigned to them, and the app keeps working where there's no signal.

The problem

A safety check on paper

Chlorine is classed as a hazardous material (hazmat), so every delivery is checked before it's unloaded: the driver, the chemical and the equipment. Before WellCheck, that check lived on paper.

Before
Paper form→Photo of the page→WhatsApp to managers→Manual copy at the warehouse
Now
WellCheck in the field→SAP + SharePoint→Chemical register, automatic

Nothing enforced at the truck

A paper form can't stop an intake when the driver isn't approved or the placards don't match.

Scattered documents

Delivery notes, licenses and certificates stayed as photos in a chat, unlinked to an order or a facility.

Everything typed twice

Once in the field, once at the warehouse, and every copy was a chance for an error.

No overview

With every intake on a photo in a chat, there was no way to see how many deliveries came in, from which supplier, and which had deviations.

In the field

Four moments shaped the interface

I met inspectors on site to check the actions they take and the conditions they work in. The inspector wants to finish the job and go home, but they're standing next to the tanks and hoses that carry the chemical. The checks protect everyone at the unloading point: the driver's protective gear is checked explicitly, and the connector, station and cleanliness checks protect the inspector too.

The turn

Make every check easier to do than to skip.

The spec I received listed five steps, about forty fields, mandatory rules and SAP mappings. It described the data, not the experience. I built the flow around the moments at the truck instead of the data types.

The flow

Short when it's fine, longer only when it isn't

The inspector always goes through the same steps, in the order the checks happen on site. The regular path stays short. Extra fields and alerts appear only when an answer points to a deviation.

Facility · Step 1

Start where you stand

Everything typed twiceScattered documents

The inspector doesn't search for the facility. The facility screen shows where they are, with the other facilities in the block below. "Start intake" opens the first step directly.

In step 1 the facility is already filled in. The inspector picks the order number, and the material, supplier and ordered quantity appear under it, so nothing from the order is typed again. The delivery note is photographed inside the report. From here on, the intake is a closed process: no app navigation bar, an X at the top to leave, and a step counter beside the title.

Step 2

When the driver isn't listed

Nothing enforced at the truck

The inspector searches the approved drivers list by name. When there's no match, the app says so, and the inspector marks the driver as not on the list. Only then do the extra required fields open: a driver's license, a chemical transport license and confirmation from the regional safety officer. Next stays disabled until all three are in.

Steps 3–5

One tap per check

Nothing enforced at the truck

Steps 3 to 5 are the safety checks: before entry, on-site safety and after unloading. Step 3 stands for all three. Each match check is one segmented choice of full, partial or no match, with no dropdown to open at the truck. A partial match opens a required safety officer field, and Next waits until an officer is chosen.

When there's no match, or no approval, the inspector can stop the intake. A sheet explains that unloading can't start, asks for a reason, a photo and a note, and sends the report to the safety officer as a rejected intake.

Step 6

See it before you send it

Nothing enforced at the truckScattered documents

Before anything is sent, the inspector sees the whole intake on one screen. Points that need attention come first: here, a partial placard match approved by the safety officer, and a driver whose licenses were attached. Each step is a card with Edit, and the received quantity is entered next to the quantity on the order, in kilograms or liters.

Sent

Sent, even offline

Scattered documentsEverything typed twiceNo overview

The confirmation shows the SAP notification number, the date, and that the documents were saved to SharePoint. In the background, the chemical register is updated automatically. With no signal, the report stays on the device and is sent automatically once the connection returns. Until then it waits on the home screen under Not sent.

Impact

What changed for the inspector

One report

No paper form and no chat. The delivery note, licenses and certificates are photographed inside the report.

Nothing to remember

The steps follow the order of work on site.

No silent deviations

A deviation needs a safety officer's approval or stops the intake, and it leads the summary.

Works offline

No signal? The report waits on the device and sends itself later.

The inspector no longer fills in a paper form or sends photos over WhatsApp. Each intake is one report, checked step by step at the truck and sent from the field.

The process is now supervised, documented and organized. Every intake is recorded in SAP and SharePoint with its documents, and the chemical register updates itself, so the information is there to use later on. For Mekorot, it's one more step from paper to digital work.
